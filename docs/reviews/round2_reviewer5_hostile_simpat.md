# Round 2, Reviewer 5: hostile SIMPAT referee report on `paper/manuscript.md`

Scope: `paper/manuscript.md` (989 lines, read in full) against `CLAIM_EVIDENCE_MATRIX.md`, `VERIFICATION_VALIDATION_FRAMEWORK.md`, `COMPARATOR_MATRIX.md`, `RELATED_WORK_VERIFICATION.md`, `FINAL_SIGNOFF.md`, `reviewer_I_simpat_manuscript.md`, `holoenergy/robot.py`, `holoenergy/decision.py`, `holoenergy/evidence/runtime.py`, the three test files, `examples/custom_robot_from_components.py`, and `experiments/results/final/*.json|csv` plus `data/evidence/final_parameter_audit.csv`. I ran the 26 tests of the three named files (all pass) and counted 355 collected tests. I did not re-run any simulation, and I did not regenerate figures. Nothing was edited except this file.

## Recommendation: MAJOR REVISION

Reviewer I recommended reject. The manuscript has improved materially: it is now explicit that it is a verification and traceability paper, it adds a decision case (EXP11), a same-mission comparison, a dynamics-evidence label, a V&V table and the equations. Its claims are mostly proportionate, and I found almost no wrong numbers (one headline number is wrong, see R5-04). But three of Reviewer I's findings are only partly answered, and each remains fatal unless addressed in a revision: no measurement anywhere (R5-01), a decision demonstration that is constructed rather than an engineering decision that changes (R5-02), and a dominant energy term whose uncertainty the method records but never propagates (R5-03). If the editor requires an empirical anchor, the correct outcome is reject.

## The 10 questions

**1. Is the framework genuinely generic or effectively BlueROV2-specific?**
Generic at the configuration and accounting layer; BlueROV2- and HoloOcean-specific everywhere that matters for the paper's evidence.
- Code: I found no BlueROV2 literals in `robot.py`; `RobotEnergyProfile` takes actuators, components, converters, battery, sources and records (`robot.py:347-443`). `runtime.py` has no vehicle constants.
- Evidence of genericity is unit tests with fabricated parts: `tests/test_generic_robot.py` uses three Blue Robotics T200 thrusters and two invented "acme_q16" thrusters with `allow_placeholders: True` and made-up tables; the example's own docstring says its numbers are "ILLUSTRATIVE ... The point is configurability, not a physical claim". It runs on an energy-only null backend, so none of the coupling (wrench allocation to dynamics, derating feedback, virtual layouts) is exercised on a second robot.
- Structural limits that make "any configurable robot" an overstatement: one payload rail, enforced by `_converters()` which raises if converters have different efficiencies (`robot.py:391-403`); wrench-allocated fixed thrusters only (no rudders, control surfaces, buoyancy engines, surface craft); Rint or energy-bucket battery only; the only dynamics backend is HoloOcean, and the only non-native layouts are "virtual" re-allocations onto the 8-thruster native body (Section 6.2), which inherit the native body's dynamics.
- All 36 native runs, the decision case, and every number in Sections 9 to 12 are one vehicle (BlueROV2 Heavy) in one simulator. The manuscript admits "other backends are not demonstrated" (13.1), but the title, abstract and contribution 1 say "configurable marine robots". Finding R5-08.

**2. Are any central values unsupported?**
Mostly supported; four problems.
- The headline closure error 1.9e-13 (abstract, Table 1, conclusion, matrix C2) is not what `energy_verification.json` contains. The maximum `worst_relative_error` over the 36 runs is 2.49e-13 (EXP5-station-c0 and EXP3-L4-2H2V-sway0.5, check `phases.sum_terminal`). 1.9e-13 is the third-largest value (R5-04).
- "52 to 100 % of mission energy is propulsion in the final missions" (5.4, C22) is false as worded: EXP5-station-c0, EXP3-L4-2H2V-sway0.5 and EXP3-L4-H45-heave have propulsion/terminal of 0 %. The "78 to 98 %" band is 77.4 to 97.6 % (scheduled inspection 1.6386/2.1164 = 77.4 %) (R5-11).
- The EXP11 numbers (0.052 Wh, 81 to 85 and 88 to 91 legs, all verdicts) were produced before the present `holoenergy/decision.py`: the file in the tree has an uncommitted, staged change (R4-01 and R4-02 fixes, modified 22:07) that is newer than `decision_case.json` (21:34), which also carries no commit or dirty stamp (R5-05).
- The "would be certifiable for 81 to 85 and 88 to 91 legs" statement drops conditions that the JSON states: it also assumes the DVL typical value is confirmed as an upper bound, and the EXP6 profile does not declare the ESC standby or tether-interface loads at all (R5-06).

**3. Are manufacturer values misrepresented as validation?**
Largely no, and the handling is better than most papers: Section 7 says "Manufacturer tables used as inputs are input evidence, not validation", Table 1 files the parameter audit as input verification, and the claim matrix lists the removed claims. Residual conflation:
- The audit grades six HoloOcean simulator literals (mass, drag coefficient, drag area, linear damping, water density, thruster clamp) plus the simulator actuator geometry as "exact", grade A. They are 7 of the 24 A records that the paper quotes as the headline "24 A". They are exact for the simulator, not for the device, and the paper itself says the drag law conflicts by 6 to 9 times with published BlueROV2 data (R5-10).
- "Grade B, representative" for bollard T200 tables in inflow is kept, and the Lam-based 7 to 9 % correction is presented as a "quantified" bias although it rests on propeller similarity, one command level and one voltage (R5-19).
- EXP9 is labelled "sub-model validation attempt" for a comparison with two published sources describing different vehicles that disagree by 6 to 9 times; "inconclusive comparison" is the accurate term.

**4. Does the evidence framework change an actual engineering conclusion?**
Not demonstrably.
- Part A: the Gemini-below-Oculus ranking is "not supported". The nominal difference is 0.038 Wh (2.1164 vs 2.1540 Wh) on a 77 s mission against a pack of 266 Wh rated; no engineer chooses a sonar on that margin, and the reversal comes from one missing converter record (the Gemini's step-up converter is unconfigured). Ping360 below both is "supported", but it is a lower-power, lower-capability device, which a datasheet already says.
- The "no configuration certifiable for 40 to 100 legs" verdict is identical for every N because the upper side is open (`decision_case.json`: INDETERMINATE for all four N and all three sonars). For N = 40 the requirement is 4.66 Wh against a nominal 2.1 Wh. The verdict reflects the rule (any unconfigured or unverified item opens the upper side), not information about the design (R5-07).
- Part B (optical below acoustic) and part C (L4-2H2V below L8-HV at 0.5 m/s) confirm the nominal answer. Part C is determined by geometry (two axial stern thrusters versus four 45-degree vectored thrusters), is robust to the drag law by construction, and L4-2H2V cannot hold sway or oblique current. None of the three parts consults a grade: `decision.py` has no reference to grades; only ranges, upper-bound flags and unconfigured items.
- Requirement verdicts keep propulsion at nominal although the paper's own EXP10 gives a factor range of 0.11 to 1.29 on it and propulsion is 77 % of that mission. The decision case was never rerun with that range. There is no baseline decision-maker (nominal plus a margin) to show what the evidence layer adds (R5-02).

**5. Are dynamics evidence and electrical evidence clearly separated?**
In reporting, yes; in substance, partly.
- Good: a separate `evidence.dynamics` block with source, external consistency, validation, and the dependent energy share (`runtime.py:58-90`, `:618-625`); the grade shares are labelled electrical-only.
- Weak: the three labels are an enum set by the profile author ("conflicting" is typed into the YAML; no computation); the block is absent from every final result file (the native runs predate commit de69564, and `dynamics_evidence` does not occur in `final_runs.json`, `crosslayer.json` or the EXP6 summary), so the "52 to 100 %" statement is re-derived from the CSV, not reported by the run; nothing from it enters intervals or verdicts; and the same registry grades the simulator-literal dynamics records A (R5-10), so the separation is undone inside the audit that C3 quotes.

**6. Does the tool comparison fairly represent competing approaches?**
No; it is favourable to the proposed tool.
- The comparators are two constant-power structures, each calibrated exactly on the calm transit and then asked about a 0.4 m/s current. A constant-power model fails there by construction. The strongest simple baseline is absent: the same T200 table applied to the same thrust trace with no battery and no evidence layer. It would reproduce HoloEnergy's propulsion exactly, which shows the current sensitivity comes from the simulator's drag, not from the method. The speed-cubic baseline was removed.
- HoloEnergy's 5.10 Wh at 0.4 m/s is itself conditional on a drag law that is 4 to 9 times above the Gabl restrained loads (EXP10 part 1: 0.11 to 0.22), so the constant-load 0.55 Wh could be nearer to a physical vehicle than 5.10 Wh. The paper concedes it cannot rank accuracy, so the comparison does not support "existing approaches cannot represent current" as a finding about tools.
- Neither tool was run; LOTUSim-Energy code was read "in part", and "Not stated" is used for it, which is correct. But Table 4 breaks its own rule: LOTUSim "Evidence in results: No" (a negative, where the table's rule requires "not stated"); DESERT, a network simulator, is scored "No" on thruster, battery, provenance and uncertainty rows; Danielis battery "No parameters given" is scored in a way that makes the row look empty. The row "Parameter provenance: every parameter linked to a graded record" is contradicted by the audit, where 10 of 54 rows have status "unlinked" (user placeholders with no record) (R5-09).

**7. Is V&V terminology correct?**
Definitions are fine; the classification is generous and the grounding is thin.
- No V&V standard or text is cited (the framework document itself says "none is cited yet"); Blattnig et al. 2008 is known only from its NTRS record. Missing: Sargent, Oberkampf and Roache, ASME V&V 20, GUM, interval-analysis and imprecise-probability sources.
- "Model verification (conservation)" and "replay equivalence = 0.0" are identities or determinism checks (bus = sum of categories; OCV = bus + loss; same code replayed). They cannot fail except by a coding error and say so only partially ("same power model").
- "Calculation verification: timestep audit" is a bare "100 Hz used and checked" with no error estimate or convergence evidence in the paper (the audit file is not cited).
- "Uncertainty and sensitivity analysis" for first-order interval propagation plus one-at-a-time factors is not uncertainty quantification; the framework document calls it "Uncertainty quantification", the paper "analysis". There is no conceptual-model validity assessment (is a Rint battery plus T200 table plus single rail adequate for the intended use?) and no operational-validity argument beyond "absent".
- The backend check is code verification of the simulator against an equation re-implemented by the authors from the same literals; it also tests an author-rebuilt unpatched build (`rebuilt-before`), not the released binary (R5-13, R5-23).

**8. Are claims proportional to evidence?**
Mostly, which is the manuscript's strength. Exceptions: (a) the abstract and Section 14 give "no payload configuration can be certified for any tested leg target" as a result, but it follows from the rule (R5-07); (b) "A measured payload-rail efficiency in the range examined would also make two of the three sonar configurations certifiable" (Section 14) omits the second required measurement and the undeclared loads (R5-06); (c) "Answer to RQ4: Yes" rests on a 0.038 Wh nominal margin, one unconfigured converter and a lower-power device; (d) "every parameter traceable to a record" versus 10 unlinked rows (R5-10); (e) four-digit energies (6.5295 Wh) persist beside a 6 to 9 times physical uncertainty (Reviewer I's I-19 not addressed); (f) RQ2/RQ3 results (Sections 9.2 to 9.5) are mostly stated to be properties of the simulated body, so their contribution to the method is nil.

**9. Could an independent researcher reproduce the central figures?**
Partly. I checked by reading, not by re-running.
- Reproducible from committed material (probable, not verified by me): FF1 to FF6 and the tables from the committed summaries and `trace10` files; the offline sensitivity and tool comparison (Reviewer J found them byte-identical on rerun); the parameter audit; the 26 tests I ran.
- Not reproducible by an outsider: all native runs need a locally patched Unreal-based HoloOcean 2.3.0 binary at `C:/HOB/packages/patched-after` (not distributed), a DESERT engine container, and an editable install of a sibling repository at a stated commit; the 100 Hz traces needed for the closure check and the EXP11 replay are not in the repository (`logs/final/full` exists only locally; archive "prepared but not deposited"). The EXP11 numbers additionally depend on a `decision.py` that is no longer the committed one (R5-05).
- Cross-layer run provenance is disclosed (dirty tree, later commits); fine, but the sibling-repo and absolute-path dependencies are not mentioned in Section 8 (R5-12).

**10. Does this make a modelling/simulation contribution suitable for SIMPAT?**
Borderline as a methods paper; weak as it stands.
- Modelling content is standard (Rint battery, table interpolation, pseudo-inverse allocation, bisection derating, interval arithmetic). The novel element is the provenance and verdict layer, whose value is asserted through three small cases that do not use grades (R5-02) and whose grades are an untested convention (Section 13.2 concedes this).
- What SIMPAT would want and is absent: a conceptual-model and operational-validity argument; a validation or at least a measured anchor; a generalisable procedure demonstrated on a second platform or backend; evidence that the grading discriminates known good from known bad parameters (inter-rater, seeded-error test) rather than being a convention.
- Contribution 9 (backend check exposing a third-party unit defect) is useful engineering but not a modelling contribution, and it has not been reported upstream (`docs/holoocean_drag_issue_draft.md`: "Nothing has been posted").
- A resubmission as an honest verification-and-traceability methodology paper, with the fixes below, is plausible for SIMPAT. As an energy-modelling paper with results, it is not.
